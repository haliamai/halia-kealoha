# Individual Research Paper — pre-deadline read

**What I read.** `docs/briefs/research-brief.md` · `prompt-log.md` · `docs/briefs/README.md` · the
repository file tree and the commit history behind the brief.

**What I did not open this pass.** Your Stage 1.x files — `analysis/`,
`capabilities/marginal-analysis/`, `docs/decisions/` — and `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `RESUME.md`,

---

Your falsification list is real, and that is rarer than it sounds. Five conditions that would each
move the conclusion rather than decorate it — and the third would end the paper outright: "Private
developers already have sufficient incentives to fund comparable resource characterization without
public support." If that holds there is no market failure, and the appropriation is subsidy rather
than correction. The fourth is what makes this an economics paper rather than an energy-policy
essay: value created publicly and captured privately is a distributional claim evidence can settle.

**The brief names more concepts than four pages can carry.** Risk and uncertainty, incentives,
opportunity cost, externalities, market failure, public versus private costs and benefits — six or
seven frameworks against roughly a thousand words. Your own hypothesis says which one or two to
keep: information as a public good, and who captures its value. Opportunity cost and incentives then
cost a sentence each; externalities enter only where they bear on who captures what.

**Decide early whether the break-even analysis is computable.** It needs four quantities: what
exploration costs a developer without public characterization, the probability of a commercially
viable find at that cost, the value of one if found, and how much the appropriation moves that
probability. The fourth is the hard one, and I do not know that it exists publicly. If it does not,
the honest paper is a stated-assumption sensitivity analysis — assumptions on the page, varied
across a plausible range — not a precise-looking figure with no provenance. Settle that in the spec
before you write.

**Graph/Chart/Diagram is its own criterion at ten percent, and a table does not satisfy it.** The
break-even framing has the chart in it already: expected public net value against the probability
improvement the characterization buys, crossing point marked. Two more curves at a higher and lower
assumed resource value make that same chart your sensitivity analysis.

**"Is it justified" is a finding, not a recommendation,** and Recommendation is the heaviest
criterion at thirty percent, scored once by me and once by your peer reviewers. Who is the
decision-maker — the Legislature deciding whether to renew, the PUC, a developer, a host community?
Pick the audience and the recommendation falls out of the finding; your fourth falsification
condition points toward one about how benefits are shared, not merely whether to spend.

**Sources.** I have not verified the appropriation, the renewable target, or the drilling scope, and
the paper should not rest on news coverage of any of them. The primaries are the appropriation item
and its authorizing language, HSEO and DBEDT documents, and USGS or state resource assessments.
Note retrieval dates.

**In order:**

1. Choose the one or two concepts the paper is actually about, and cut the rest from the brief.
2. Decide computable break-even versus stated-assumption sensitivity, and write that into the spec.
3. Name the decision-maker; the recommendation follows.
4. Pull the primaries — appropriation language, HSEO/DBEDT, USGS or state assessment — with
   retrieval dates.
5. Sketch the break-even chart from whichever numbers survive step 4.
