# Perfect Competition — Engagement Brief

## The Problem
The farm needs to decide how many of its 64 beds to use for tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun to maximize profit. There are several factors to consider, including the return on each crop, labor requirements, diminishing returns, and planting limits.

## What I Am Assuming
I’m assuming we should try to use all 64 beds and diversify the crops rather than rely too heavily on one. However, I’m also assuming the model may show that using all 64 beds isn’t actually the most profitable option.

## Hypothesis
I predict 20 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 24 mesclun beds. I think tomatoes may have the largest return, but the cost of labor could impact how many beds are actually worth planting.

## How I Would Know I Was Wrong
I would know I was wrong if the model shows that a different mix of crops would generate more profit, especially if labor costs make some of my predicted beds more expensive to produce than what they earn.
